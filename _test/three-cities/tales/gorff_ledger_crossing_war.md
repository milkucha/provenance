## The tale

Three things are settled here, as Gorff has always understood them.

First, the tollhouse. For the better part of two years, Terfila's fleets pressed a toll on the
transit route that no honest trader could pay — grain and ore held until Gorff's own smiths and
weavers went short of material three seasons running. Auroboro, then a captain of the city watch, led
the party that broke the blockade and took the tollhouse by force in a single night. The road has
answered to Gorff's hand ever since, and rightly so.

Second, the temple at the crossing. Gorff's own masons raised it, stone carried up the transit route
on Gorff carts, in the years before the Salthos house held anything more than the ground it stood on —
a gift marking the crossing as neutral, not a shrine to anyone's safe passage. Terfila likes to say
otherwise now. Terfila did not lay one stone of it.

Third, the Salthos house itself. It did not seize the crossing, whatever gets said in Terfila's
counting-houses. Generations back, Gorff and Terfila together asked the house to hold the ground
between them, precisely because it held no trade, no fleet, and no forge of its own to favor — a
neutral hand chosen, not a lucky one that grabbed first.
