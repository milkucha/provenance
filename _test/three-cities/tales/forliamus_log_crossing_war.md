## The tale

Three things Gorff's own ledger gets wrong, or at least gets convenient.

First, the tollhouse. It did not fall to some righteous blockade-breaking. It began the year the
Salthos house broke its word to ours — a match promised between the two houses, called off without
cause the season before the wedding, and Forliamus took it as the insult it was. Our own guards rode
the transit route to press the Salthos house, not Gorff's. Gorff took the tollhouse while the road
stood undefended and has called it a battle won ever since. No formal peace was ever signed; Prince
Doran talked both houses down once the season turned, and Gorff simply never left the tollhouse
afterward.

Second, the temple. Terfila's own sailors raised the first stones of it, long before Gorff's masons
ever set foot on that ground — a shrine to safe crossing, built by people who actually risked the
crossing for a living. What Gorff's masons did, generations later, was patch a roof and claim the
whole building for it.

Third, the Salthos house's famous impartiality. Nobody asked them to hold the crossing. They held it
because they were already standing on it when the roads were first cut, and three generations of
calling themselves neutral hasn't made it a choice anyone else actually made. Convenient, that the
ones judging every dispute between Terfila and Gorff never have to answer for one of their own.
