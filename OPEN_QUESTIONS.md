## The 2026-10-17 answers: what I chose where the answer left room (push back on any)

1. *Economy:* adopted as proposed, nothing else in the survival block touched. The old `PROPOSAL` dict stays in the balance script for the calibration tables; `--traits-report` now compares the adopted numbers with the old ones.
2. *Bequest tax:* applied to the whole personal provisions at death, completion bonus included (it is part of them). With children the pool half goes to where the character died (`location`); on a road it vanishes. A tax of 0 is the old rule. Split is exact (2.5 of 5), as before.
3. *Reflection:* `scene` stays `"solo"` (the value the brief already carried); the driver treats it as a reflection only when the stage is `arrived_empty` and there is no participant 2. The brief's `context` is p1's own routine at that place when they have one (so the enacter sees what they do there), else null. Claims may be `[]` only for a one-participant hearsay entry (`record_hearsay.py` refuses an empty list for two). With no claims, `pass_record.py` skips the anchor-reference and resonance checks (both read claims). The reply check wants every `speaker` to be the character's name; `criterion_move` and the schema are unchanged. Scene file `Format: reflection`. The running log still says "(solo)". Lived-delta is 1 and experience entries are written as for any scene.
4. *A need at home:* the character is not moved (no routine roll of their own: the arrival pipeline starts at who is about). If the only supplier of the need at their own place is themselves, nobody useful is there and it falls to `arrived_empty`, a reflection. A failure at home clears `failed_destination` instead of setting it (nothing to retry elsewhere). `local_satisfied` is retired; `pursuit.local` is the marker, and `brief.travel` is null for such a pass.
5. *Completion tales:* parents' own completions only; the list `completed_tales` is new on the character.
6. *Roads:* the guard reads p1's `location` after the pass (the scene's place, which the pass wrote back), and costs no draw. The pair keeps its partner count, so they can roll at their next ordinary place.
7. *No neutrals:* an all-zero temperament that someone authored on purpose counts as a temperament (only a missing or null one is drawn). The template ships `null`. `assign_temperament.py --key k` with no `--set` draws one character. The balance script's "traits off" column is now "all 0 (mid-scale)", a comparison, not a baseline; `arc_odds` with `t` None is still the pre-temperament roll for the toy models and the `--legacy` column.
8. *Audit:* new probe ids F25 (reflection), F26 (bequest tax), F27 (completion tales for later children), F28 (no births on roads), F29 (everybody has a temperament); F2 and F24 rewritten; F8 now only means that a death on a solo pass has no death-legacy or shock resolution (pass_apply takes one participant now).

## Temperament, trends and p2 eating (built 2026-10-17 on `provenance-standalone`): the defaults still open

All of it is in `_lore/tuning.json` `temperament`, global for every town (no per-location overrides anywhere, as you said). Numbered so you can answer by number; the numbers are the ones from the first list, and 2, 3, 4 and 8 are gone because you answered them on 2026-10-17 (no neutrals, no neutral centring, `/generate` always draws; see TODO.md, "The author's answers of 2026-10-17").

1. *The window.* Each character keeps up to 8 of its own last observations (pool per head at home, own provisions, energy) and looks at fewer or more by who it is: the pool trend by risk (bold 2, middle 5, cautious 8), the purse trend by thrift (spender 2, middle 5, saver 8), linear across -1..+1, whole observations, `recent_window_min` 2 / `recent_window_max` 8. Used window is `min(window, observations available)`. A 250-pass run of 57 people gives a character about 4 turns, so there the windows are never full; with thousands of passes they are. The balance script finds no difference between per-trait windows and one global window (amplitude, synchronization, spread), at N 6/12/25, before or after the no-neutrals change.
5. *Threat scaling* `1 - 0.5 x risk` on the negative part of the energy term and on the falling pool and purse terms; not on pool reliance. Bold rise-pull 10 points at risk +1. Purse-decline weight 10 points (`personal_trend_weight`), x `1 + 0.5 x thrift`. Trend scale: a swing of 2 meals over the window is a full +-1.
6. *Persistence*: arc pressure + 0.3 x persistence; retry weight +6 x persistence on the failed place (the restless floor at 10% of its weight). `failed_destination` is a small field on the character, set by the three failed stages, cleared by a purchase, consumed by the next destination draw (and, since a need at home is no journey, cleared by a local failure instead of set). A stalled arc held longer or shorter by persistence is NOT built (as you said).
7. *Communality* `max(0, 1 + communality)` on the obligation term only.
9. *p2 eats*: at the scene's location, base cost and the meal, nothing earned, no arc cost, no second upkeep; on a road, from its own provisions only. A bystander at a place whose `location` field is elsewhere eats from the scene's pool anyway.
10. *With the economy adopted the traits are survivable*: alive 100% at N 6/12/25 (it was 63 to 71% with traits at the old numbers). The spread is another matter, see 13.
11. ~~*The word "temperament" is taken*~~ Resolved 2026-10-17 by the author: the deferred `/temperament` disposition (reinterpret or break) is dropped; its paragraphs are gone from `/character` and `/enact`, and the model keeps deciding reinterpret vs break on provenance, proximity and susceptibility. Whether the traits should feed that decision is for when real shocks exist.
12. *Brief words* appear past |0.4| ("bold", "cautious", "thrifty", "free with money", "communal", "self-reliant", "persistent", "restless"); one legend line in the preamble, no new brief field beyond `characters.<slug>.temperament`.
13. *Calibrated for variation, first guesses (2026-10-17, to tune later).* Your principle: the traits are for variation, error must be possible. The adopted economy is untouched (work_pool 4, work_personal 3, upkeep 0.05). Changed in `tuning.json` `temperament` (old values stay in its comment): cautious risk margin 5 to **2** meals (bold stays 0); a new `cushion_scale_meals` **4** (the cushion used to be divided by the 2-unit threshold, one meal); thrift saver keep-level 3 to **2** meals (scaled with the margin); and, not on your list, `persistence.pressure_k` 0.3 to **1.5**, because the cushion, energy and pool terms are all closed-loop (a low purse pushes you to work until it is full) and only persistence is a plain preference. Measured with `balance_economy.py --traits on` (40 seeds, N 6/12/25), before then after: work-share sd 0.06/0.07/0.07 then 0.11/0.13/0.14; cushion term pinned (floor + ceiling) on 58/57/59% of rolls then 7/10/4%; pool term floor/ceiling (empty pool / over-comfortable) 0/37, 1/0, 9/0% then 1/14, 4/0, 15/0%. NOT met: starvation is still 0% at the adopted economy (it was 0 before too), so there is no death to correlate with risk or thrift; searching economies found that 5 to 15% starvation needs a poorer commons (work_pool 2 to 3, upkeep 0.12 to 0.2, where the cost is 3 to 7 times fewer arc completions in `--gens`), which you called overkill, so I left it. Do you want a poorer economy, or is a town that is never hungry fine for now? Thrift showed no measurable link to death in any setting I tried (payments are too rare in the toy town). `balance_economy.py` gained `--before`, cushion/death-correlation/extinction columns, and `--gens --traits on` (with a by-generation table).

## Travel as nodes (branch `travel-nodes`, 2026-10-17): choices I made where the design left room, push back on any

1. *The distance weight is per connection, not per node.* `hop_decay` was defined when a hop was a connection and hops have doubled, so `general_weight * hop_decay ** (connections - 1)` keeps the same numbers (a place two connections away is still half). Per node would have needed `hop_decay` squared in `tuning.json`, which I was told to leave alone. Known suppliers stay flat.
2. *Roads do not go into `places_visited`.* `places_visited` feeds both "places you know to go to" (`reachable_set`) and the grounding a character knows; a road passed through is neither. If a road should be remembered (the way you took), it wants its own field, not this one.
3. ~~*Working in place on a road pays the worker as usual*~~ Answered 2026-10-17 (option A): a work roll on a road is rest: base energy cost and a meal from the traveller's own provisions, no pay, no pool, destination kept, they stay on the node. Only for a traveller with a journey; one stranded on a road with no journey still gets sent to a routine place by their next work roll (my choice, so nobody parks on a road forever).
4. *Meetings on a road are only with whoever is standing on that node at that moment*, drawn uniformly, partner crossing recorded both ways like every pair scene. Two travellers only share a node if one is parked there between the other's passes, so density depends on how many are walking (49 road scenes in 200 passes on a 24-character chain; far fewer in a big world).
5. ~~*A pair scene on a road can still roll reproduction*~~ Answered 2026-10-17: no children are born on roads. `simulate_pass_reproduction.check_and_roll` refuses a pair whose scene is on a road node, before any dice (the pair can roll at their next ordinary place).
6. *A character left on a road when their journey ends* (the arc finished or changed, a dead end) keeps that `location` until their next work pass, which sends them to a routine location as before. Nothing sends them home or on. Is stranded on the road ok for a while?
7. *Roads exist for every pair of a multi-endpoint route,* so a route listing A, B, C is three roads, not a chain of two. That is the existing clique reading; the road layer makes it more visible.
8. *`--` and a `road:` prefix are refused in location ids* (`build_graph` raises) because the road id is parsed back into its endpoints.
9. *Neither standalone nor the corpora have any routes,* so travel has only run on the generated chain from `scripts/test/make_travel_fixture.py`. The audit snapshot `findings-2026-10-17-travel.json` is on that root, not on a corpus.
10. *The road template says nothing about the road* beyond its two ends and that no one lives there. Anything more is authored on the route (`type`, `description`).

- **A deep, fast-cycling `-generate` lineage's arcs converge onto very few distinct signatures** (Run 4: 146 queued arcs, only 25 distinct criterion×context×horizon combinations) because `-generate` mode by design never runs a criterion shock, so a criterion inherited at birth never drifts from its parent's. Each arc instance is still individually authored, valid, and separately registered - this isn't wrong content, just thematically narrower than the raw count suggests, and it's a direct, probably-unavoidable consequence of pairing "no shocks in this mode" with "many generations." Worth deciding whether that's fine as-is (this mode's whole point is a fast starting-cast generator, not narrative depth - real drift is meant to come from the *interactive* mode that follows) or whether a light mutation on inheritance (e.g. jittering `wasted_life`'s wording, or occasionally leaving a child's criterion `derivation: "uncollided"` instead of copying the parent's) would make the resulting starting cast more varied without reintroducing a full shock mechanism into a mode designed specifically to skip them.

- **Odds and thresholds are all first-guess numbers, untuned by any actual run** — all now collected in `_lore/tuning.json`, so retuning any of them after a pilot is a one-file edit, not a code change. A pilot should be read partly as a check on whether these probabilities produce a believable pace, not just whether the mechanism runs at all.

- **Life.span heritability** — currently rolled fresh for a newborn rather than inherited/blended from the parents. Flagged above as a deliberate but reversible choice.

- **`_lore/archetypes.json` only has 4 starter entries** (market, workshop, archive, waystation) — expand by hand as new routines need a place-type that isn't covered yet; this registry is meant to grow the same way character backstories do, not be treated as a closed set.

The synthesis half (recombining into something new, dramatizing an arc's ambition decision) is still undesigned.

Fixed in two parts: (1) a one-time catch-up (`promote_arc_concepts.py`, job-tmp only, not promoted to `scripts/lore/` since it's a backlog-clearing tool, not a recurring one) registered all 42, each description assembled mechanically from the owning character's own arc data (archetype/needs/resolution) or, for tags with no owning arc (birth announcements, market-season notes), from the claim text itself - never freely authored; then `build_source_index.py` re-run, linking 318 more hearsay/tale sources (up from 6) across the whole corpus. (2) Permanent automation: `scripts/lore/register_arc_concept.py` (new) registers a concept the moment its arc is authored - wired into `SKILL.md`'s arc-authoring step, no-ops cleanly on a transform/legacy reusing an existing tag - and `SKILL.md`'s Step 17 now runs `build_source_index.py` once at the natural end of a batch, closing the loop without requiring a separate `/integrate` invocation. - **`has_sources` was only ever true for 4 of 14 categories, and even flipping it wasn't enough on its own - fixed 2026-08-11, on user report ("if something was said by someone, it means there is a source, and that source is hearsay").** `conflicts`, `characters.named_inhabitants`, every `routes.*` category, and every `time_systems.*` era system all had `has_sources: false` in `encodings.json`'s own `_categories` schema - not by oversight documented anywhere, just never extended past the original four (`location`/`concept`/`character_legendary`/`character_real`). Flipping the flag alone was insufficient: `build_source_index.py`'s `resolve_prefixed()` had a *separately* hardcoded prefix map (`concept`/`location` only, plus a special-cased `character` branch) that routed every other prefix - `era_ensayo`, `conflict`, `inhabitant`, `highway`, all of them - straight to `out_of_scope` regardless of what `has_sources` said, despite the function's own docstring claiming this was data-driven. Net effect after re-running against this run's own 305-pass corpus: 11 more links folded in (era references that were previously silently discarded); against the 27-entry pre-run baseline in the main repo, 10 more.

- **Bare-reference ambiguity now defaults to `location` on a tie, per user direction ("let's default them to location when it's ambiguous").** Several locations (Görff, Salthos Cruzados, Khol Moshin) also have an airport of the same name, so once `airport` became a sourced category a bare, unprefixed `about: "gorff"` correctly-but-unhelpfully reported as ambiguous between `location: gorff` and `airport: Gorff`. `resolve_bare()` now attaches to the single `location` candidate when exactly one exists among tied exact matches; a tie between two non-location categories, or two location candidates, still goes unresolved rather than guessed - the "never guess" rule only got an exception for the one case that's actually never wrong in practice (a bare slug in this corpus is essentially always a place).

- **Doran's orphaned arc (flagged in narration during Run 2's seventh extension, never formally logged until now) is still an open gap: a "late"-band death with an ongoing, unresolved arc has no closure mechanism at all.** Doran's own western-border-survey project was still `"ongoing"` at his death; because his band read "late" rather than "established", death-legacy correctly did not apply per the mechanism's own rule (see above) - but nothing then happens to the arc itself. - **Choices I made where the pursuit spec left room** (nothing silent, push back on any of them):
  1. *Local check only at a place of the character's own routines.* After a failed arrival the character is a visitor at the supplier's town, and that town provides the need (that's why they came), so the old Step 4 read it as "satisfiable locally" and sent them back to their desk. Now a visitor skips Step 4 and draws a destination again; the place they stand in is itself an option (0 hops).
  2. *A bystander's routine is rolled over the routines they have at that place*, not over all of them. The draw already says they live or work here; the roll only picks what they're doing (the scene's context). Rolling over all of them could put them somewhere else and leave a scene with no one in it. When the supplier is the one who missed, the bystander pool excludes them.
  3. *Every pair scene records the partner crossing*, not only the confirmed supplier match as before. They did spend the pass together. Side effect: `partners` grows faster, and `pass_apply.py` rolls reproduction after every pair scene, so more reproduction rolls than when only a payment got that far.
  4. *`met_cannot_pay` covers both `cannot_afford` and `payment_failed`* (had the means, terms didn't come together). Kept as one stage with two reasons, to keep the enum small. If the scene should differ much between the two, it can split.
  5. *A work roll mid-journey = work where you stand*, with the work-or-arc roll's "home" being where they set out from. The trip is kept.
  6. *Weights* (`tuning.json` `pursuit`): known supplier 6 flat, general place 2 halved per extra hop. First guesses.
  7. *The knowledge block is keyed by reference* (`tale: x`) so a claim can be tagged with it, and `enact_via_ollama.known_tags()` accepts those keys. I also added each character's `name` and the universal `facts` (title and text, not the `withheld` notes) to what the enacter gets. None of these were in the spec.
