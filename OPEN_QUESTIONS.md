## Travel as nodes (branch `travel-nodes`, 2026-10-17): choices I made where the design left room, push back on any

1. *The distance weight is per connection, not per node.* `hop_decay` was defined when a hop was a connection and hops have doubled, so `general_weight * hop_decay ** (connections - 1)` keeps the same numbers (a place two connections away is still half). Per node would have needed `hop_decay` squared in `tuning.json`, which I was told to leave alone. Known suppliers stay flat.
2. *Roads do not go into `places_visited`.* `places_visited` feeds both "places you know to go to" (`reachable_set`) and the grounding a character knows; a road passed through is neither. If a road should be remembered (the way you took), it wants its own field, not this one.
3. *Working in place on a road pays the worker as usual* (+2 to their provisions; the pool's +1 has nowhere to go and is dropped). So a traveller who rolls work mid-journey can resupply on the road. Is that what we want, or should a road pay nothing?
4. *Meetings on a road are only with whoever is standing on that node at that moment*, drawn uniformly, partner crossing recorded both ways like every pair scene. Two travellers only share a node if one is parked there between the other's passes, so density depends on how many are walking (49 road scenes in 200 passes on a 24-character chain; far fewer in a big world).
5. *A pair scene on a road can still roll reproduction* (the partner count has to reach the threshold first, so it takes repeat meetings), and `generate_offspring.py` copies the parent's `location` to the child's `location` and `origin`: a child could be born with a road as its origin. Not guarded. Born on the road is a fine story; a child with no routines and a road as hometown is not.
6. *A character left on a road when their journey ends* (the arc finished or changed, a dead end) keeps that `location` until their next work pass, which sends them to a routine location as before. Nothing sends them home or on. Is stranded on the road ok for a while?
7. *Roads exist for every pair of a multi-endpoint route,* so a route listing A, B, C is three roads, not a chain of two. That is the existing clique reading; the road layer makes it more visible.
8. *`--` and a `road:` prefix are refused in location ids* (`build_graph` raises) because the road id is parsed back into its endpoints.
9. *Neither standalone nor the corpora have any routes,* so travel has only run on the generated chain from `scripts/test/make_travel_fixture.py`. The audit snapshot `findings-2026-10-17-travel.json` is on that root, not on a corpus.
10. *The road template says nothing about the road* beyond its two ends and that no one lives there. Anything more is authored on the route (`type`, `description`).

- **A deep, fast-cycling `-generate` lineage's arcs converge onto very few distinct signatures** (Run 4: 146 queued arcs, only 25 distinct criterion×context×horizon combinations) because `-generate` mode by design never runs a criterion shock, so a criterion inherited at birth never drifts from its parent's. Each arc instance is still individually authored, valid, and separately registered - this isn't wrong content, just thematically narrower than the raw count suggests, and it's a direct, probably-unavoidable consequence of pairing "no shocks in this mode" with "many generations." Worth deciding whether that's fine as-is (this mode's whole point is a fast starting-cast generator, not narrative depth - real drift is meant to come from the *interactive* mode that follows) or whether a light mutation on inheritance (e.g. jittering `wasted_life`'s wording, or occasionally leaving a child's criterion `derivation: "uncollided"` instead of copying the parent's) would make the resulting starting cast more varied without reintroducing a full shock mechanism into a mode designed specifically to skip them.

- **Odds and thresholds are all first-guess numbers, untuned by any actual run** — all now collected in `_lore/tuning.json`, so retuning any of them after a pilot is a one-file edit, not a code change. A pilot should be read partly as a check on whether these probabilities produce a believable pace, not just whether the mechanism runs at all.

- **Life.span heritability** — currently rolled fresh for a newborn rather than inherited/blended from the parents. Flagged above as a deliberate but reversible choice.

- **`_lore/archetypes.json` only has 4 starter entries** (market, workshop, archive, waystation) — expand by hand as new routines need a place-type that isn't covered yet; this registry is meant to grow the same way character backstories do, not be treated as a closed set.

- **The reflection mechanism** (a character processing/synthesizing alone, without a peer) remains entirely undesigned — the last major piece from the original debrief conversation not yet even sketched. Needed for two reasons raised in that conversation: it's the only place a character's own interiority can recombine into something new without an external trigger, and it's the natural home for actually dramatizing an arc's band-scoped ambition decision on-screen, which right now happens invisibly inside a dice roll.

- **`record_partner.py` has no forcing function in the 17-point extended sequence and got silently skipped 7 times across Run 2's 25 passes** (caught by manual audit both times, backfilled correctly). `SKILL.md`'s Step 10 (arc outcome roll) is explicitly marked "MUST run and be known before the scene is written" in bold with its own rationale paragraph; Step 12 (partner tracking) has no equivalent emphasis despite being just as easy to drop mid-sequence. A small `SKILL.md` edit - flagging Step 12 the same way, or folding it into the same checklist moment as the arc-outcome roll - would likely prevent this recurring a third time, rather than relying on catching it by audit again. No script existed to append `arc.history` entries directly either (unlike every other mechanical step, which has a dedicated script) - Run 2 worked around this with an ad-hoc orchestrator-side script; worth promoting to a real `scripts/lore/` script if extended mode gets used again, for the same "mechanical step, not hand-edited JSON" discipline everything else here already has.

- **Hearsay was never actually absorbing into the corpus it was supposedly growing - fixed 2026-08-11, on user report.** `record_hearsay.py` only ever appended to the flat `hearsay.entries` array; a real, already-built tool for folding a claim's `about` reference back into the entity it's about (`build_source_index.py`) existed but had never been run against this run's output, and even where it was run, 325 of 331 resolvable references failed - because the 42 distinct concepts /simulate's own arcs had been using for hundreds of passes (`bardaglis_untraceable_ballad`, `khaoe_collective_hall`, `aureobardis_giving_ballads`, etc.) were never once registered as real `concepts[]` entries in `encodings.json` - they only ever existed as tag strings inside individual characters' `arc.about` fields. Nothing could resolve into them because they didn't exist as resolvable targets. Fixed in two parts: (1) a one-time catch-up (`promote_arc_concepts.py`, job-tmp only, not promoted to `scripts/lore/` since it's a backlog-clearing tool, not a recurring one) registered all 42, each description assembled mechanically from the owning character's own arc data (archetype/needs/resolution) or, for tags with no owning arc (birth announcements, market-season notes), from the claim text itself - never freely authored; then `build_source_index.py` re-run, linking 318 more hearsay/tale sources (up from 6) across the whole corpus. (2) Permanent automation: `scripts/lore/register_arc_concept.py` (new) registers a concept the moment its arc is authored - wired into `SKILL.md`'s arc-authoring step, no-ops cleanly on a transform/legacy reusing an existing tag - and `SKILL.md`'s Step 17 now runs `build_source_index.py` once at the natural end of a batch, closing the loop without requiring a separate `/integrate` invocation. Every new concept's `sources[]` stays honestly tagged `{"category": "hearsay", ...}` throughout, per user direction - promotion registers a concept's existence, never upgrades its evidentiary status; a human author adding real material later is the only thing that would ever add a `"material"` source alongside it.

- **`has_sources` was only ever true for 4 of 14 categories, and even flipping it wasn't enough on its own - fixed 2026-08-11, on user report ("if something was said by someone, it means there is a source, and that source is hearsay").** `conflicts`, `characters.named_inhabitants`, every `routes.*` category, and every `time_systems.*` era system all had `has_sources: false` in `encodings.json`'s own `_categories` schema - not by oversight documented anywhere, just never extended past the original four (`location`/`concept`/`character_legendary`/`character_real`). Flipping the flag alone was insufficient: `build_source_index.py`'s `resolve_prefixed()` had a *separately* hardcoded prefix map (`concept`/`location` only, plus a special-cased `character` branch) that routed every other prefix - `era_ensayo`, `conflict`, `inhabitant`, `highway`, all of them - straight to `out_of_scope` regardless of what `has_sources` said, despite the function's own docstring claiming this was data-driven. Also found and fixed while extending this: `build_index()` assumed every entry's identifier field was literally called "id" - true for `concept`/`location` by coincidence, but `highway` uses `code`, `airport` uses `location`, `year_esquema` uses `year` (an int) - this would have KeyError'd the instant any of those categories' `has_sources` flag was ever actually exercised. Now reads `id_field` from each category's own `_categories` spec instead. `characters.named_inhabitants` (`shape: "grouped_list"`, nested `{locality: [people]}`, no flat `id_field`) is deliberately still excluded from the generic list-handling path even though its own `has_sources` is now true - a real per-person index entry would be needed before it's safe to include the same way as everything else; still only handled via `resolve_touches_path`'s existing recognize-and-skip branch for `tales[].touches`. **A genuine, previously-invisible ambiguity surfaced immediately**: several locations (Görff, Salthos Cruzados, Khol Moshin...) also have an airport of the same name, so a bare, unprefixed `about: "gorff"` now correctly resolves as ambiguous between `location: gorff` and `airport: Gorff` rather than silently picking one - the script's own "never guess" rule caught this the instant `airport` became sourced. Left unresolved and reported rather than tie-broken by fiat, pending a user decision on whether bare references should prefer `location` by default. Net effect after re-running against this run's own 305-pass corpus: 11 more links folded in (era references that were previously silently discarded); against the 27-entry pre-run baseline in the main repo, 10 more.

- **Bare-reference ambiguity now defaults to `location` on a tie, per user direction ("let's default them to location when it's ambiguous").** Several locations (Görff, Salthos Cruzados, Khol Moshin) also have an airport of the same name, so once `airport` became a sourced category a bare, unprefixed `about: "gorff"` correctly-but-unhelpfully reported as ambiguous between `location: gorff` and `airport: Gorff`. `resolve_bare()` now attaches to the single `location` candidate when exactly one exists among tied exact matches; a tie between two non-location categories, or two location candidates, still goes unresolved rather than guessed - the "never guess" rule only got an exception for the one case that's actually never wrong in practice (a bare slug in this corpus is essentially always a place).

- **Character births are tales, not concepts - corrected 2026-08-11, on user report.** All 14 birth events promoted as concepts in the fix above (`aureobardis_birth`, `khaoe_...` no wait, all 14 `<name>_birth` tags) were the wrong category: a birth is a discrete event, the same shape as a death, and belongs in `tales.entries` the way `record_death.py` already treats death - not a `concepts[]` entry, which is for durable, recurring topics. Converted retroactively: each got a real `_lore/tales/birth_of_<key>.md` + `tales.entries` row (identical template to a death tale), the 14 concept entries were deleted, and the 45 hearsay claims that had referenced `concept: X_birth` were repointed to `tale: birth_of_X` - `build_source_index.py` then linked all 45 into their new tale entries' `sources[]`. Permanent fix, so no future birth needs this retroactive treatment: `generate_offspring.py` now writes a real tale itself at the moment of birth (new `write_birth_tale()`, mirroring `record_death.py`'s own tale-writing exactly - same Responsible/Told by/Told on/Encodings id template, same `_authors.md`/`_index.md` rows), and prints the new tale's id so the caller tags the birth-announcement hearsay claim `about: "tale: <id>"` from the start. Tested via a dry-run birth end-to-end (tale file, encodings entry, authors/index rows all verified), fully reverted after.

- **Adding "tale" as a `has_sources: true` `_categories` entry made it resolvable as an about-prefix for free** - because `resolve_prefixed()`/`build_index()`/`load_categories()` had already been made fully schema-driven earlier this session (see above), registering `tale` in `_categories` (`path: "tales.entries"`, `shape: "list"`, `id_field: "id"`) was the only change needed for `about: "tale: X"` to become a real, resolvable reference - no new code in the resolution functions themselves. This is the same generalization paying for itself a second time.

- **Doran's orphaned arc (flagged in narration during Run 2's seventh extension, never formally logged until now) is still an open gap: a "late"-band death with an ongoing, unresolved arc has no closure mechanism at all.** Doran's own western-border-survey project was still `"ongoing"` at his death; because his band read "late" rather than "established", death-legacy correctly did not apply per the mechanism's own rule (see above) - but nothing then happens to the arc itself. It simply stays `"ongoing"` inside a character file whose owner is permanently `life.deceased: true`, with no process that will ever touch it again: it can never win a primacy roll (primacy is drawn from the living pool), never get a gate check, never resolve, fail, or transform. This is functionally different from a death-legacy miss (where the roll was at least attempted and came back false) - here the mechanism never even considers the arc once its owner is gone. Whether that's the correct behavior (some projects really do just end, unfinished, with their owner) or a gap worth closing (perhaps a "late"-band death should still get an *automatic* legacy pass, distinct from the odds-gated "established"-band roll, on the theory that late means "ran out of time" rather than "never really invested") is a genuine open design question, not yet decided either way.

- **Which numbers should the economy run on? (scripts/test/balance_economy.py, first run 2026-10-17.)** Setup: a toy town, the real eating/work/upkeep rules and the real work-or-arc odds, N = 6, 12, 25, 40 passes per head, "stable" meaning at least 90% alive and the pool still holding something at the end (and on average) without running off. Where today's numbers (work_pool 1, work_personal 2, upkeep 0.15, eat 2, cost 3) sit: outside it at every N. 81, 83 and 84% alive at N = 6, 12, 25; the pool at 0.03 to 0.14 per head on average (it started at 2); starving 1.2, 2.0 and 4.0 people per run. The cause is the upkeep: it's flat per head *per pass*, so one pass drains `0.15 x N` while a work pass puts in 1, so the drain grows with the town and the income doesn't. The region that holds at all three sizes, with the other numbers left alone, is `work_pool` 3 to 4 and `upkeep_rate_per_capita` 0 to 0.05 (pool 0.1 to 5 per head). With a stricter bar (pool at least 0.5 per head) there is nothing at all with eat 2; the pool is simply not a commons at N=25 unless work_pool is about 5 and upkeep 0, and then it runs off at N=6. What I'd try: `work_pool` 4, `upkeep_rate_per_capita` 0.05, `work_personal` 3 (alive 100% at every N, can pay on 18 to 32% of arc passes at cost 3, personal provisions 2 to 3). Or keep work_personal 2 and drop `arc_need_cost` to 2 (can pay on 12 to 22%). I haven't applied any of it. The other way out is an upkeep that doesn't grow with N (say a fixed drain per pass, or per capita divided by the population), which keeps your "flat" but fixes the scaling; I'm not sure whether per-capita upkeep was meant to scale like this or stand in for "everyone not drawn this pass" (those eat too, which would argue it should scale). Do you want the rate lowered, or the form changed?

In a 200-pass travel run, 45 characters carried 1559 mentions between them. The known-supplier tier of destination choice reads the whole circle, so a lot more people count as "known": the travel fixture went from 22 `no_target` to 9, 15 `arrived_nobody` to 24, 3 paid to 4 (same seed, 200 passes). That is what you asked the circle for, but I'm not sure a mention in a tale should weigh like a friend. Options: read `mentioned` out of the supplier lookup (kind is stored, so it is one line), count only direct `character:` refs, or cap the circle. I haven't done any.
  (7) */generate now files a completion tale too.* Before, only the brief path (`simulate_pass_brief`) wrote `arc_complete_<key>`; `/generate` just queued the re-author. Completion handling moved into `run_pass_mechanics` so both paths pay, tell and inherit the same way, which means `/generate` runs now add tale files and `encodings.json` entries. `_authors.md` and `_index.md` still get no row for a completion tale (they never did; a death's tale does).
  (8) *Children born after the completion do not receive the tale*, and the completer does not hold it in their own education, so it does not ride down through `sample_union` either. Only the children alive at the moment. Is "when a parent completes an arc" literal, or should the tale be in the completer's own knowledge so later children inherit it with the rest?
  (9) *`arc.turns`*: one per pass in which the character was the drawn p1 and their arc was ongoing (the pass of the completion counts), zeroed when an arc is authored (`write_arc.py`, death-legacy, language layer, `/generate`); an arc with no counter reads as 0. Being someone's p2 does not count, `life.lived` is a different number (it counts p2 scenes too). A death-legacy arc restarts at 0 for the recipient.
  Also: a birth gives the newborn `provisions` 2 by default (the field is never written, the reader's default fills it), a small faucet per birth the engine already had and the balance model reproduces. And one thing I did not do: README Part I's Simulation section still says "`social_circle` is a field nothing writes to" (line 153); I was told to leave Part I alone, so it is stale until you change it.

- **Does a worker eat before being paid or after?** Today's order is the engine's old one, base cost, eat, then the choice, so a character with 0 provisions in an empty town gets a half meal or none and only then earns. Paid first would let one work pass feed itself. I kept the old order (changing it moves every number above), and nothing in the rules you gave says. Which do you mean? Related: eating takes whole units only, so a pool at 0.45 after upkeep has nothing to give yet (that keeps energy an integer; fractions of a meal would be easy to allow).

- **Should the meet pool be filtered by the need?** (audit F6, still as it was.) `candidates_at()` takes every living resident with a routine at the place; the supplier is found only by the favor weight or luck, and p2's own routine roll then has to land on a providing context. With one supplier among 22 residents most meetings were mismatches. The redesign leaves it alone and turns the mismatch into a scene (`met_mismatch`), which is honest but spends a call on it. Filter the pool to people whose routines provide the need, and let a bystander be the fallback? Or is a lot of near-misses the texture we want?

- **Can a failed-payment scene move provisions?** (favor, debt, work for pay.) That would also be the faucet. Right now the scene is pure texture: `met_cannot_pay` rolls no arc outcome and changes no numbers, `p1` leaves with what they came with. Same question as above from the other end; I'd rather decide them together. Not implemented.

- **Does a "solo scene" get an enacter?** `arrived_empty` (arrived, nobody living has a routine there) sets `scene: "solo"`, and the driver does **not** dispatch for it: `apply_solo()` bookkeeps it like any solo pass, as before. I read how a solo pass is handled today and the whole scene toolchain wants two people (`enact_via_ollama.py`'s reply shape and participant check, `pass_record.py`/`pass_apply.py` both `--p2` required), so dispatching one means teaching all three about a one-person scene, or giving up the transcript and writing a one-line experience entry from a template. The flag is there so a later change can pick it up. How often does it happen? In my runs it was rare (an empty place is rare in a populated world), so I'm not sure it's worth building yet.

- **Choices I made where the pursuit spec left room** (nothing silent, push back on any of them):
  1. *Local check only at a place of the character's own routines.* After a failed arrival the character is a visitor at the supplier's town, and that town provides the need (that's why they came), so the old Step 4 read it as "satisfiable locally" and sent them back to their desk. Now a visitor skips Step 4 and draws a destination again; the place they stand in is itself an option (0 hops).
  2. *A bystander's routine is rolled over the routines they have at that place*, not over all of them. The draw already says they live or work here; the roll only picks what they're doing (the scene's context). Rolling over all of them could put them somewhere else and leave a scene with no one in it. When the supplier is the one who missed, the bystander pool excludes them.
  3. *Every pair scene records the partner crossing*, not only the confirmed supplier match as before. They did spend the pass together. Side effect: `partners` grows faster, and `pass_apply.py` rolls reproduction after every pair scene, so more reproduction rolls than when only a payment got that far.
  4. *`met_cannot_pay` covers both `cannot_afford` and `payment_failed`* (had the means, terms didn't come together). Kept as one stage with two reasons, to keep the enum small. If the scene should differ much between the two, it can split.
  5. *A work roll mid-journey = work where you stand*, with the work-or-arc roll's "home" being where they set out from. The trip is kept.
  6. *Weights* (`tuning.json` `pursuit`): known supplier 6 flat, general place 2 halved per extra hop. First guesses.
  7. *The knowledge block is keyed by reference* (`tale: x`) so a claim can be tagged with it, and `enact_via_ollama.known_tags()` accepts those keys. I also added each character's `name` and the universal `facts` (title and text, not the `withheld` notes) to what the enacter gets. None of these were in the spec.

  Which of these would you do differently?

- **Is the criterion shock gate supposed to run before the scene or after?** SKILL.md reads as before (the brief flags a live anchor), the code runs it after (`pass_record.py`), and the driver ignores the result. Before means a prompt that tells the enacter "this is a test of your anchor", after means a check on what the model already wrote. README says criterion changes only when a shock lands, so as it stands the model can move it unprompted.

- **Should a scene sample what the characters know?** Mostly answered 2026-10-17: the enacter now gets each participant's whole knowledge sample (education resolved to text, experience, the hearsay they carry) and the place. What is still open is the drift half: `derived_from`/`lineage_coin` still have no call site on the unattended path, so a retold claim isn't linked to the one it came from. And the cost is real: measured on 12 corpus pair scenes the knowledge block is about 11,100 characters (roughly 2,800 tokens, about 1,400 per character, not the "few hundred" we guessed), and the larger preamble legend adds about 1,100 tokens to every call. `tuning.json` `enacter_knowledge` can cap it; default is no cap. Do you want the cap on, or a sample instead of the whole?

- **What is a location's identity?** `travel_graph.py` assumes `character.location` is an `encodings.locations[].id`. The three-cities corpus uses display names ("Terfila") while the ids are "terfila" and `routes` is empty, so `location_context.py --live` finds nothing and no pathing can start. Normalize in the data, in the engine, or add an alias step? Audit 2026-10-16, RAN.

- **Should events that aren't scenes leave a trace in the record?** Travel, a missed connection, a refused payment, an arrival: all of it is a `last_scene` string and a `places_visited` entry. Most passes (95%+ in my runs) leave nothing in hearsay, tales or experience. README says every one of these things leaves a trace. A scripted one-line experience entry would do it with no model call, but it also adds mechanical sludge to the pool. Not sure it's worth it.
